title: Build vs. buy for a 20-person company

summary: Off-the-shelf software is almost always the right answer. Here are the three situations where it isn't.
---

I build custom software for a living, so take this with appropriate suspicion: most small businesses should buy, not build. Off-the-shelf tools are cheaper, better supported and improve without you paying for it.

But "almost always" leaves room, and I keep seeing the same three exceptions.

## 1. The way you work is the product

If customers choose you because of how you schedule, quote or deliver, forcing that process into someone else's software means sanding off the thing that makes you different. A mechanical contractor that promises two-hour arrival windows needs a dispatch board that understands two-hour windows.

## 2. You're paying for five tools to do one job

A surprising number of teams run a CRM, a scheduling app, a form builder and two automations just to book a job. Each one is reasonable on its own. Together they cost more than a small custom tool and fail in more interesting ways.

## 3. The glue is the problem

Sometimes every tool is right and the only thing missing is the connection between them. That's the cheapest custom work there is, and the one I recommend most often.
