---
title: Pricing fixed-fee work without losing your shirt
topic: Business
summary: I quote almost everything at a fixed price. Here's how I keep that from becoming a bet against myself.
---

Small business owners hate open-ended invoices, and they're right to. So I quote fixed prices for nearly everything. The trick is making the fixed part small enough to estimate honestly.

## Fix the release, not the project

I never quote a whole system. I quote the first release, which discovery has already described screen by screen. Everything after that gets its own price once we've both seen the first release in use.

## Price the unknowns separately

Every project has one or two things I can't estimate: an undocumented API, a decade of messy data. I name them in the quote and price them as a separate line with a cap. Clients appreciate seeing the risk instead of having it buried in a padded number.

## Know your own multiplier

I keep a log of estimated versus actual hours for every release. Over four years my estimates run about 1.3 times low, consistently. So I multiply by 1.3. It isn't elegant, but it works.
