---
title: Training is the launch
topic: Process
summary: Software that nobody uses hasn't launched. Why I budget as much for training as for the final week of development.
---

I used to think of launch as the day the software went live. Now I think of it as the day the last holdout stops using the old way.

## Train in the room where the work happens

At a veterinary practice last year, I scheduled training in the conference room. Nobody retained anything. The second round happened at the front desk, during a slow Tuesday afternoon, with real appointments. It took half the time and stuck.

## Pick a champion, not a manager

Every team has one person others ask when they're stuck. That person gets trained first, a week early, and gets my phone number. They will answer more questions in the first month than I will.

## Plan for the second week

The first week, everyone is careful. The second week, they're busy again, and that's when shortcuts and workarounds appear. I stay close for at least three weeks, and I schedule a short check-in at the end of week two specifically to find them.
