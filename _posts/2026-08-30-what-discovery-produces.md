I charge for discovery, and I tell clients they can walk away with the plan and hire someone else to build it. A few have. Most don't, but the option keeps me honest about what goes in the document.

## The deliverable

At the end of two weeks, the client gets a written plan of about fifteen pages. It always has the same parts:

1. **How work moves today.** A plain-language map of each workflow, written so the office manager would agree with every sentence.
2. **Where it hurts.** The three to five bottlenecks, with a rough cost for each in hours or dollars per month.
3. **The first release.** The smallest piece of software that removes the worst bottleneck, described screen by screen.
4. **What comes later.** Everything else, roughly sized, in the order I'd tackle it.
5. **Cost and schedule.** A fixed price for the first release and a range for the rest.

## What it deliberately leaves out

No architecture diagrams, no technology choices beyond a sentence or two, and no feature lists the client didn't ask for. Those belong in my notes, not in a document a business owner has to read on a Sunday.

## Why fixed fee

Discovery is where scope gets decided. If I'm paid by the hour during the phase where I'm also deciding how much work there is, the incentives point the wrong way. A fixed fee removes that question from the room.
