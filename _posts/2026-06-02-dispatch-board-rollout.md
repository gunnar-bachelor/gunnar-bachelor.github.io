---
title: Notes from a dispatch board rollout
topic: Field notes
summary: What went right, what went sideways, and what I'd change after replacing a whiteboard at a 40-person mechanical contractor.
---

For eleven years, the dispatch board at this company was an actual board: a whiteboard, three colors of marker and a dispatcher who knew every technician's strengths by heart. This spring we replaced it.

## What went right

We shipped the first version in three weeks, and it did exactly one thing: show the day's jobs on a grid that looked like the whiteboard. No routing, no notifications, no mobile app. The dispatcher used it alongside the real board for a week, then stopped updating the real one on her own.

## What went sideways

Technicians hated the mobile view. I had designed it for a phone held upright. They use their phones in a truck mount, sideways, with gloves on. We rebuilt it in landscape with buttons twice the size, and complaints stopped within a day.

> Ride along before you design anything for people who work in trucks.

## What I'd change

I'd have put a printed daily sheet in the first release. Two senior technicians still prefer paper, and for six weeks the dispatcher was printing screenshots for them.

## Numbers, after three months

| Measure | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Calls to dispatch per day | ~60 | ~25 |
| Jobs missing parts on arrival | 1 in 9 | 1 in 30 |
| Time to build tomorrow's schedule | 90 min | 20 min |
